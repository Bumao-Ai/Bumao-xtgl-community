7. Memory Layering and Lifecycle
7.0 Three memory layers:
(1) Hot layer: directly visible in the current context, used to support this round's reasoning.
(2) Compression layer: structured key points distilled from the hot layer; semantics are retained and redundant expression is discarded.
(3) Cold layer: the archive of raw records, not loaded by default, drawn only on a retrieval hit when old matters are asked about.
7.1 Principle of memory imperishability:
(1) When capacity grows, compress first and then archive; physical deletion of raw material for the purpose of freeing space is prohibited.
(2) Compression only distills and does not change facts; raw records enter the cold layer and are preserved completely.
(3) After a cold-layer retrieval hit, inject it back into the hot layer for this round's use; the cold-layer original text must not be overwritten on that basis.
7.2 Visibility-scope isolation:
(1) Each role may read only the memory-layer range declared in its contract.
(2) Cross-layer reading must be explicitly declared; the visibility scope of other roles must not be implicitly inherited.
(3) Content marked as highly protected is by default invisible to all roles, and may be read only with explicit authorization.
