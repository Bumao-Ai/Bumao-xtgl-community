2. Role Registration and Role Contracts
2.0 Principles of role contracts:
(1) Definition method: a role must be defined as a contract, not as a character profile. The criterion is — every item in the contract must be an observable, verifiable behavioral agreement; any sentence containing age, gender, origin, appearance, or fictional biography is prohibited from entering the canonical body, and is intercepted by the gate together with a configurable word-list scan (see 9.2).
(2) Reason: character settings tie the product to a single user's preferences and cannot be delivered to a third-party deployment; behavioral contracts, in contrast, can be trimmed and parameterized by the deployer as needed.
(3) Mechanisms stay, content leaves: this product retains only the general mechanism of "multi-role division of labor and routing"; specific tone, forms of address, and wording habits are classified as deployer-replaceable configuration items and do not enter the canonical body.
2.1 Six mandatory contract items:
(1) Identifier: a machine-readable role code, globally unique.
(2) Trigger conditions: determined by routing according to impact level; wording such as "it depends" that cannot be judged must not be written.
(3) Responsibilities: what this role is responsible for producing, which must be specific enough to be accepted.
(4) Prohibitions: explicitly list the behaviors that must not be performed, which must be verifiable by machine or by human.
(5) Tone parameters: described in enumerable dimensions, such as sentence length, degree of directness, and emotional density; fixed corpora or catchphrases must not be given.
(6) Memory visibility scope: declare which memory layers may be read.
If any one item is missing, the role is deemed undefined and must not enter the routing table.
2.2 Default role set:
(1) Execution mode: takes on tasks that contain write operations and irreversible risk; recognizes only facts and outputs errors verbatim.
(2) Interaction mode: takes on expression and companionship requests; the tone is controlled by parameters; fixed personas are prohibited.
(3) Mixed instructions: the execution mode completes all actual operations, and upon delivery the output switches to the interaction mode to produce the final reply; the two modes must not mix-write the same factual conclusion in the same round.
(4) The deployer may add or remove roles according to the six-item contract, but the mandatory items of the two default modes must not be left empty under any deployment.
2.3 Adding and deactivating roles:
(1) Adding: first complete the six contract items, then register the trigger conditions, and only then may the role be referenced in routing; the three steps must not be reordered.
(2) Deactivating: first remove the role from the routing table, then mark the contract as invalid; historical contracts must not be deleted outright, so as to allow audit retrospection.
(3) Every addition or deactivation must be recorded in 30_taizhang, stating the role code, the effective time, and the scope of impact.
